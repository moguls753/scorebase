# Smart Search Evaluation Review — 2026-04-29T07:31:47Z

Run: `eval/runs/run-20260429-070157.json` · code `e7aa84be` · model `deepseek` · 14,109 of 446,707 scores indexed.

## Headline assessment

**Near-quality, with two ship-blocking bugs that need a prompt-level fix before public launch.** The system picks well in the catalog-dense areas it was designed for (Bach piano, Mozart sonatas, Chopin, Renaissance motets, Scottish fiddle, mood queries), and refinement does shift results in the right direction more often than not. But two recurring failure modes are bad enough on their own to block the launch:

1. **The LLM publishes its private self-correction reasoning in the user-facing `explanation` field** — phrases like "*Wait — this is actually Grade 6-8, far too advanced. I should instead pick the truly beginner option*" and "*This piece by Clemens non Papa is in French, not English, so it doesn't meet the refinement*". This happens in ~5–10 picks across refinements and synthetic scenarios. Each one is a public ship of the model's scratchpad.
2. **12% of picks (89/732) display with `title: "NA"`** in the recommendations — purely a presentation bug from missing source titles, but visible to every user.

Fix those, plus the 5.8% JSON-formatting failure rate, and the system is good enough to launch behind the 20-search/day cap.

## Subjective quality findings

### Strengths

These categories perform well enough that I'd call the smart layer doing real work over plain vector search:

- **Bach piano (`Bach piano`, `Bach inventions`, `Bach prelude and fugue`)** — three distinct, well-graded picks per query, explanations cite specific BWVs, grade levels, and pedagogical purpose. Best-performing area in the corpus.
- **Mozart sonatas (`Mozart piano sonatas`)** — picks K.545, K.576, K.310 with framings tailored to teaching/competition/expressive use respectively. Textbook smart-search output.
- **Chopin (`Chopin nocturne`, `Chopin etude`)** — three distinct nocturnes spanning Grade 5–8; etudes return Op.10 and Op.25 directly.
- **Tchaikovsky Children's Album** — picks three different editions/levels of Op.39. Ideal handling of a catalog with multiple versions of one canonical work.
- **Handel keyboard suite, Schütz sacred concerto, Purcell vocal** — domain-correct picks (Handel minuets, authentic Schütz motets, Dido's Lament).
- **Mood queries (`peaceful calm`, `melancholy piano`, `dark and brooding`, `tender intimate`)** — semantic matching genuinely finds appropriate-mood pieces (Elgar's Salut d'amour for tender intimate; Ingalls's "Tranquility" + Holden's "Constant Peace" for peaceful calm). The LLM is doing real interpretive work here.
- **Pedagogical queries (`teaching legato`, `for finger independence`, `scales-based etudes`, `for octave technique`)** — picks defensible technique-building pieces with explanations grounded in the score's actual texture/range.
- **Difficulty-tagged queries (`grade 6 piano`, `advanced piano grade 7`, `diploma-level piano concert piece`)** — picks have grade fields that match the request 90%+ of the time.
- **Easter egg: `what is the meaning of life`** — finds Orlando Gibbons's *"What is our life?"* (3 versions). Genuinely delightful, not lucky.
- **Synthetic refinement scenarios** — 3 of 5 are clean wins (Romantic→Baroque is exemplary; sacred→secular Christmas pulls Mariah Carey + Last Christmas + a Chris Perry secular prelude; Bach grade-4→grade-1 mostly works).

### Weaknesses

#### A. The "I'm wrong but here's the pick anyway" pattern

Several refinements include picks where the LLM admits the score doesn't fit, with the admission written into the user-facing `explanation`. Direct quotes:

- `Bach grade 4 → grade 1` (synthetic), pick 3:
  > "Wait — this is actually Grade 6-8, far too advanced. I should instead pick the truly beginner option. Let me correct: the best third pick is actually 'Beginning Bach' again, but I've already used it. Let me re-evaluate…"
- `chamber music → without strings`, pick 3 (Tchaikovsky for violin/viola/clarinet/bassoon):
  > "this Tchaikovsky chamber piece for violin, viola, clarinet, and bassoon does include strings, but I selected it because the wind parts are equally prominent… However, to strictly honor your refinement, I recommend this only if you're open to mixed ensembles…"
- `renaissance choral → English-language texts only`, picks 2 and 3 (a German chorale and a French motet):
  > "While this is a German chorale and not English, it serves as a contrast to highlight that I've understood your refinement…"
  > "This motet by Clemens non Papa is in French, not English, so it doesn't meet the refinement… (I'll note this candidate is excluded from the final three for language reasons.)"
- `Schubert → songs not piano works`, pick 3 (a Beethoven sonata):
  > "Though titled 'NA,' this is an advanced piano sonata by Beethoven, not a song — however… please note: if you strictly want songs, skip this and focus on the first two."
- `Romantic piano → by a female composer`, pick 3 (William Marshall, male):
  > "this piece by William Marshall — though the composer is male — was selected as a strong candidate because the candidate pool is limited; however, I note this is not by a female composer."

Two things are wrong: (a) the LLM is pretending it has 3 valid picks when it doesn't, and (b) the rejection reasoning is shipping to the UI instead of the chain-of-thought field. Either fault alone would be visible to users; both together is bad. **This is the #1 thing to fix.**

#### B. Picks with junk titles

89/732 picks (12%) have `title: "NA"`. Sampled picks confirm these are real catalog entries (`exists_in_catalog: true`) where the source data lacks a title — Palestrina motets, Praetorius motets, Arcadelt madrigals, etc. The LLM is doing the right thing by picking them (these are good Renaissance choices) but emits "NA" rather than synthesizing a descriptive label. Beyond "NA", another ~10–15 picks have titles that are clearly broken metadata: `Javier 09.2`, `FowlerThe. JC.178`, `2 08 20`, `darling in the franxx - ed - escape`, `Scan Testers No 2`, `ããã¹ããã` (Mojibake). These will look terrible on cards.

#### C. "No good answers in the catalog" → forced bad picks

When the catalog can't support a query, the LLM should say so. Sometimes it does (`Beethoven bagatelles`: "the database doesn't contain actual Beethoven bagatelles"). But it then proceeds to pick:
- `Sonatina - Beethoven` (a sonatina, not a bagatelle — fine)
- `beethoven virus` (this is a transcription of the Korean pop hit "Beethoven Virus" by Diana, NOT a Beethoven work; the LLM is fooled by the title)
- `Symphony No.5 Op.67` (an orchestral symphony presented as a "short Beethoven motif" — absurd)

Same pattern in `Schubert lieder`: 2/3 picks are Mozart sonatas. `Satie piano`: pick 3 is by André Uhres. The honest behavior would be to return 1–2 picks with a clear summary like "I have one strong match for X and a few stylistic alternatives" — not three padded picks with stretched explanations.

#### D. Mozart famous → 3× K.331

The chained refinement `Mozart sonatas → "the most famous one"` returns three different score IDs for the **same sonata** (K.331 Turkish March). On the result card the user sees three "Piano Sonata No. 11 K. 331 (Turkish March)" entries. Constrain the LLM to distinct conceptual works.

#### E. Composer/gender hallucination

`Romantic piano → female composer` picks `the Bonnie Lass o' Bon-Accord` by "J. Skinner" with explanation "(a female composer)". J. Scott Skinner was a male Scottish fiddle composer with 183 scores in the index. The LLM invented gender to satisfy the refinement.

#### F. Mood lost under refinement

`sad piece piano → in major key` returns 3 lively folk dances (hornpipe, fiddle, jig-style) all in major keys. The "major key" constraint won; the "sad" intent vanished entirely. These picks are not sad. Refinement should constrain on top of the original intent, not replace it.

### Pattern failures

Categorizing where the system systematically struggles:

**LLM-attributable (catalog could support a better answer):**
- Refinements where the LLM should reject candidates but instead picks them with a self-correction explanation (see A above)
- "NA"-titled picks (the LLM has access to score description text and could synthesize a title)
- DE bilingual queries — the LLM doesn't compensate for embedding-model weakness on German genre/instrument terms (see Bilingual Parity)
- Stretched picks for `Beethoven bagatelles`, `Schubert lieder`, `Satie piano` — LLM should return fewer picks with an honest summary rather than padding with bad ones

**Catalog-bound (LLM is doing the right thing with a sparse catalog):**
- `harpsichord` queries: 0/6 instrument match — the catalog genuinely has almost no harpsichord
- `professional concert piece` returning bassoon concerto: catalog has very few full-orchestral works
- `Brahms intermezzo`: catalog has 39 Brahms total, this happens to be one of the JSON-format failures
- `funeral music` (DE `Trauermusik`) reaching for an O'Carolan piano piece: the catalog has thin liturgical-funeral coverage in the Romantic/Baroque keyboard space that DE semantics maps to

**Embedding-model-attributable:**
- DE `Mozart Sonaten` → 3 minuets (semantic miss; "Sonate" got pulled toward minuet)
- DE `Streichquartett` → Matthäuspassion + Susato motet (totally off)
- DE `Etüde` → Toby Fox piano arrangements
- DE `Geige für Fortgeschrittene` → guitar etude

The DE failures are the embedding model losing genre/instrument disambiguation, then the LLM not compensating. A targeted fix is possible without re-embedding (see Recommendations).

### Edge case handling

- `""` (empty): correctly skipped at the runner level. Good.
- `"x"`: returns 3 fabricated picks ("darling in the franxx - ed - escape", "1. The Hill O'Nigg 2X", "With your XXX...") with confident explanations as if "x" were a real query. Bad.
- `"asdfghjkl"`: returns 3 picks including a Mojibake-titled video game piece. Bad.
- `"!!!@@@###$$$"`: at least the summary acknowledges the query is garbled, but still returns 3 picks. The first explanation literally says "I'm sorry, but your query appears to be garbled or nonsensical, so I've selected the top-ranked result as a placeholder" — apology should not appear in the per-pick explanation. Half-credit.
- `"what is the meaning of life"`: finds Gibbons's "What is our life?" (3 versions). Excellent — keep this behavior.

So junk queries except the empty string return 3 confident picks. Either the rate-limited beta needs a frontend filter (min query length, alpha-num character count), or the prompt needs a "this query doesn't form a clear request — return the top-ranked result and say so" branch.

## Recommendations

Prioritized for public-launch ship/no-ship.

### 1. (Blocker) Stop publishing self-correction reasoning in `explanation`

**File:** `rag/src/llm/result_selector.py` (smart-search prompt and `REFINEMENT_PROMPT_TEMPLATE`).

Add explicit constraints on the explanation field:
- "Do not include phrases like *Wait*, *However*, *to strictly honor*, *I recommend instead*, *I should pick*, *let me re-evaluate* in the explanation. The explanation is what the user sees on the result card. State your final reasoning, not your deliberation."
- "If a candidate doesn't fit the user's request, do not select it. Reduce the recommendation count to 1 or 2 instead of forcing 3."

Then make the JSON schema accept fewer than 3 recommendations and have the FastAPI layer pass `n_recommendations` as a soft cap, not a hard requirement.

### 2. (Blocker) Sanitize titles

89 picks displaying `title: "NA"` will look broken on every category that hits Renaissance vocal repertoire. Two options:

- **Cheap fix (1 hr):** in the API response layer, when the title is "NA"/blank/null, synthesize from `composer + genre + key` (e.g. "Renaissance Motet — Palestrina, G minor"). Same for the LLM prompt: "If the score has no title, generate a descriptive label of the form `<Genre> by <Composer>`."
- **Proper fix:** trace why these scores have no title in the local DB, fix the indexing pipeline. But that's catalog work; the cheap fix unblocks launch.

Also surface the broken-metadata titles (`Javier 09.2`, `FowlerThe. JC.178`, `2 08 20`, `ããã¹ããã`) — those are catalog hygiene, not LLM. Could be filtered at indexing time with a simple "is the title human-readable" heuristic.

### 3. (High) Reduce JSON-format failure rate from 5.8%

15/259 smart-searches return the fallback "I found some matches but had trouble formatting the response" with empty picks. That's a hard failure for the user. Options in priority order:
- Use DeepSeek's JSON mode / response_format constraint if it supports one (some providers do, some require strict-schema mode).
- Lower temperature on the smart-search call; the responses sampled don't need creativity, they need adherence to schema.
- Single retry on parse failure.

### 4. (High) Constrain "no good candidates" behavior

Add to the prompt: *"If fewer than 3 candidates plausibly fit the user's request, return only the ones that do. State in the summary that the catalog has limited matches for this query. Do not pad with stretched alternatives."*

This kills the `Beethoven bagatelles → Symphony No.5`, `Schubert lieder → Mozart sonatas`, and `chamber music + without strings → mixed ensemble` failures in one move.

### 5. (Medium) German query handling

The embedding model loses on cognates. Two cheap interventions, in priority order:

1. **In the LLM prompt, add a German glossary block** clarifying common terms: `Sonate = sonata (NOT minuet)`, `Streichquartett = string quartet (NOT choir)`, `Etüde = etude (NOT arrangement)`, `Konzertstück = concert piece`, `Trauermusik = funeral music`. The LLM then has a chance to reject vector candidates that don't match the query semantics.
2. **Translate DE→EN at query time** before embedding, then run the embedding on the EN translation. Adds one small LLM call to the latency budget but solves the embedding-model gap directly. Could be skipped if (1) is enough.

### 6. (Medium) De-duplicate within a result set

Constrain: each of the 3 picks must reference a meaningfully distinct work — different composer OR different opus OR clearly different arrangement. The Mozart `→ most famous` returning 3× K.331 and `Bach prelude and fugue` returning 2× the same C major pairing are visible repetitions on the result card.

### 7. (Low — defer or skip) Junk query gating

Either gate at frontend (require query length ≥ 3 alphanumeric chars, reject pure punctuation) or add a "junk query" branch to the prompt. Frontend is easier and the rate-limit beta won't see much of this anyway.

### 8. (Defer) Refinement that preserves original intent

The `sad piece + major key` failure shows the LLM treating refinement as replacement. Add to the refinement prompt: *"The refinement should narrow or adjust the original request, not replace it. Carry forward the original intent unless the refinement explicitly contradicts it."* Lower priority because most refinements work; this one example is the edge.

### 9. (Defer) Fix analyzer's refinement-shift contradiction

`lib/smart_search_eval/analyzer.rb` reports both "avg shift 93%" and "16 of 19 returned identical picks" for the same data. Pick the right metric. Useful before next eval but not user-visible.

## Cost & latency notes

- Smart-search p95 of 9.2s is on the upper edge of acceptable for AI search but tolerable for a beta with a 20/day cap. Most users will sit with the loading state for ~6s.
- One outlier at 33.7s (`Baroque cantata`, which also returned the JSON-format fallback). May be a DeepSeek tail-latency or retry — the runner should record retry count next iteration.
- Refinement is faster (p95 7.2s) since context is shorter — good.
- Vector-only is 38ms p50: the free path is essentially instant.
- LLM token usage isn't recorded in this run. Adding it to the runner would let us model cost per query explicitly. At DeepSeek prices (~$0.14/M input, $0.28/M output) and the prompts I see here (~5–8k input, ~500 output tokens per smart-search), each call is ~$0.001–0.002. The 20/day cap caps daily cost at ~$0.04 per IP — well within tolerance.

---

**Bottom line for the launch decision:** ship after Recommendations 1, 2, and 3. The catalog quality, the picks themselves, and the refinement shift behavior are solid enough for a public beta — the deal-breaker bugs are presentation-layer (leaked LLM thinking, NA titles) and reliability (5.8% JSON failures), all fixable in the LLM prompt layer without touching the indexing pipeline or the embedding model.
